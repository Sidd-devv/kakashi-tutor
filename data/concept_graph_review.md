## Definition of Life Processes  (`life_processes_definition`, section 5.1, pages [80]-[80], recall)
Life processes are the maintenance functions that keep living organisms alive, even when they are not active.

**Needs first:** nothing

**Must stay true:**
- Life processes are necessary for maintenance and prevention of damage and breakdown in organisms.  
  _"The processes which together perform this maintenance job are life processes."_
- Energy for life processes comes from outside the body, usually as food.  
  _"This energy comes from outside the body of the individual organism. So there must be a process to transfer a source of energy from outside the body of the organism, which we call food, to the inside, a process we commonly call nutrition."_

**Common misconceptions:**
- `life_processes_only_when_active`: Life processes only happen when an organism is active.

## Need for Energy and Raw Materials  (`need_for_energy_and_raw_materials`, section 5.1, pages [80]-[80], understanding)
Organisms require energy and raw materials from outside to maintain life and grow.

**Needs first:** Definition of Life Processes

**Must stay true:**
- Energy is needed to prevent damage and breakdown in organisms.  
  _"Since these maintenance processes are needed to prevent damage and break-down, energy is needed for them."_
- Growth requires additional raw materials from outside the body.  
  _"If the body size of the organisms is to grow, additional raw material will also be needed from outside."_

**Common misconceptions:**
- `energy_only_for_movement`: Energy is only needed when the organism is moving.

## Overview of Nutrition  (`nutrition_overview`, section 5.2, pages [81]-[81], recall)
Nutrition is the process by which organisms obtain energy and materials from food.

**Needs first:** Need for Energy and Raw Materials

**Must stay true:**
- Food provides both energy and materials for growth and maintenance.  
  _"This source of energy and materials is the food we eat."_
- All organisms require energy and materials, but obtain them in different ways.  
  _"The general requirement for energy and materials is common in all organisms, but it is fulfilled in different ways."_

**Common misconceptions:**
- `nutrition_only_for_energy`: Nutrition is only for getting energy, not materials.

## Autotrophic Nutrition  (`autotrophic_nutrition`, section 5.2.1, pages [81]-[81], understanding)
Autotrophic nutrition is when organisms make their own food from simple substances like CO2 and water using sunlight.

**Needs first:** Overview of Nutrition

**Must stay true:**
- Autotrophs convert carbon dioxide and water into carbohydrates using sunlight and chlorophyll.  
  _"This material is taken in the form of carbon dioxide and water which is converted into carbohydrates in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll."_
- Photosynthesis is the process by which autotrophs meet their carbon and energy requirements.  
  _"Carbon and energy requirements of the autotrophic organism are fulfilled by photosynthesis."_

**Common misconceptions:**
- `autotrophs_do_not_need_raw_materials`: Autotrophs do not need any materials from outside.

## Events in Photosynthesis  (`events_in_photosynthesis`, section 5.2.1, pages [82]-[82], understanding)
Photosynthesis involves absorption of light, conversion to chemical energy, splitting of water, and reduction of CO2 to carbohydrates.

**Needs first:** Autotrophic Nutrition

**Must stay true:**
- Desert plants may take up CO2 at night and use it during the day.  
  _"For example, desert plants take up carbon dioxide at night and prepare an intermediate which is acted upon by the energy absorbed by the chlorophyll during the day."_
- Photosynthesis happens in three events: chlorophyll absorbs light energy; light energy is converted to chemical energy and water is split; carbon dioxide is reduced to carbohydrates.  
  _"(i) Absorption of light energy by chlorophyll. (ii) Conversion of light energy to chemical energy and splitting of water molecules into hydrogen and oxygen. (iii) Reduction of carbon dioxide to carbohydrates."_

**Common misconceptions:**
- `photosynthesis_one_step`: Photosynthesis is a single-step process.

## Role of Chlorophyll and Stomata in Photosynthesis  (`role_of_chlorophyll_and_stomata`, section 5.2.1, pages [82]-[83], understanding)
Chlorophyll is essential for photosynthesis, and stomata regulate gas exchange in leaves.

**Needs first:** Events in Photosynthesis

**Must stay true:**
- Chlorophyll is found in chloroplasts and is essential for photosynthesis.  
  _"These green dots are cell organelles called chloroplasts which contain chlorophyll."_
- Stomata are tiny pores on leaves that allow gas exchange for photosynthesis.  
  _"Massive amounts of gaseous exchange takes place in the leaves through these pores for the purpose of photosynthesis."_

**Common misconceptions:**
- `all_cells_have_chlorophyll`: All plant cells have chlorophyll.
- `stomata_always_open`: Stomata are always open.

## Types of Heterotrophic Nutrition  (`types_of_heterotrophic_nutrition`, section 5.2.2, pages [84]-[84], understanding)

**Needs first:** Heterotrophic Nutrition

**Must stay true:**

## Nutrition in Unicellular Organisms  (`nutrition_in_unicellular_organisms`, section 5.2.3, pages [84]-[84], understanding)

## Digestion and Absorption in Small Intestine  (`digestion_and_absorption_in_small_intestine`, section 5.2.4, pages [86]-[86], understanding)

**Needs first:** Digestion in the Stomach

**Must stay true:**

- Food from the stomach is acidic, so it is made alkaline (by bile juice from the liver) for the pancreatic enzymes to act.  
  _"The food coming from the stomach is acidic and has to be made alkaline for the pancreatic enzymes to act. Bile juice from the liver accomplishes this in addition to acting on fats."_

**Common misconceptions:**
- `absorption_in_stomach`: Absorption of nutrients happens mainly in the stomach.

## Nephron and Urine Formation  (`nephron_and_urine_formation`, section 5.5.1, pages [97]-[97], understanding)
Each kidney filters blood in many tiny units called nephrons; useful substances are taken back as the urine flows on.

**Needs first:** Excretion in Human Beings

**Must stay true:**
- The kidney's filtration units are nephrons: a cluster of thin-walled blood capillaries joined to the cup-shaped Bowman's capsule, which collects the filtrate.  
  _"Each capillary cluster in the kidney is associated with the cup-shaped end of a coiled tube called Bowman’s capsule that collects the filtrate (Fig. 5.14). Each kidney has large numbers of these filtration units called nephrons packed close together."_
- Glucose, amino acids, salts and much of the water in the first filtrate are selectively re-absorbed as urine flows along the tube.  
  _"Some substances in the initial filtrate, such as glucose, amino acids, salts and a major amount of water, are selectively re-absorbed as the urine flows along the tube."_
- About 180 L of filtrate forms daily but only a litre or two is excreted, because the rest is re-absorbed.  
  _"However, the volume actually excreted is only a litre or two a day, because the remaining filtrate is re-absorbed in the kidney tubules."_

**Common misconceptions:**
- `all_filtrate_becomes_urine`: Everything the kidney filters out of the blood leaves the body as urine.

## Urine Storage and Regulation  (`urine_storage_and_regulation`, section 5.5.1, pages [97]-[97], recall)

**Needs first:** Nephron and Urine Formation

**Common misconceptions:**
- `urine_amount_fixed`: The kidneys make the same amount of urine whatever the body needs.
